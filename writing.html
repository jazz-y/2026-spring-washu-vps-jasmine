Writing Exercise 1

One of my most important routines is my skincare routine. Besides helping my skin stay healthy and balanced, I feel that it helps me wash away the past; I wash away the heavier moisturizing products in the morning and the stresses of the day at night. I enjoy the textures, consistency, and smell of the products I use. Whether I am cleaning my face or using toner, the rubbing motions on my face comfort me; it’s a form of therapy too. I always get satisfaction out of it, even though my skin isn’t always perfect. It’s fun to do and is a form of self-care for me a the end of the day; it feels good and does good.

Writing Exercise 2

One thing that brings out the best in me is my dog, Fluffy. Having a dog is kind of like having a child, in a way, but somehow also very different. Whatever mood I am in when I get home, my dog is always happy to see me, looking at me with his soft, brown eyes and wagging his fluffy tail; I can’t stop but smile and pet him and his soft, curly fur as he approaches me eagerly and jumps into my lap for pets and cuddling. When Fluffy lays down next to me on the couch as I work on homework, I love to stroke his soft fur, give him head scratches, and give him belly rubs, inspiring me to keep working. Even at night when he curls up to sleep in my bed, I tend to stay up less because I am more mindful of him getting more sleep. It’s funny that I care more about his sleep schedule than my own. Even when he causes trouble, especially when he was a puppy, I can’t stay angry at him for long; I have become a lot more tolerant to undesirable scenarios and handling them.

Writing Exercise 3

I really cherish making handmade corn tortillas when I’m at home. Anyone can make tortillas, but to master making them you need time and patience. There are so many things that can go wrong; you need to prepare the dough to right the moisture or they’ll tear or be too thick; you need to make the dough balls the right size or they’ll be too small or too big; You need the griddle at the right temperature for the tortillas to puff up like a pita; you need to perfect your technique for laying them on the griddle, or else they’ll break/warp; you need to flip them at the right time so they puff; you need to cook them just right so they’re not raw or burnt; you need to be careful to not burn yourself on the griddle. Surely, you can buy tortillas from the store, but they don’t taste or feel the same. We eat tortillas with every traditional meal when I’m home, often as a replacement for cutlery; good tortillas can really elevate a dish.

Writing Exercise 4

I grew up right during the age of transition from the early internet to the modern internet, the rise of smartphones, and social media. The first times I used a computer were the few times my class got to go the the computer lab and use what looked like chunky, colorful iMacs with Windows installed. At home, I had game consoles and a TV, but I spent plenty of time outside and playing in my neighborhood. The point being, I didn't really grow up with the heavy presence of the internet - it didn't have a big influence during my childhood as it does in our lives now. I have fond memories of running around the neighborhood, playing with kids around my age until sunset. We would play ball games and run around the field of my local high school and in the empty parking lot. I remember excitedley planning with my neighbor to watch the latest episode of a cartoon at her house, waiting for it to air on the TV. Random kids would knock at the door and ask to play - I definitely scraped my knees a couple times playing tag, but it was fun. Even though I'm a computer science student and love a lot of what I do, I kind of miss those times when the internet wasn't so present in my life and I could just be a kid. I feel like kids these days (boy do I sound old right now) are always on their phones and social media, wanting to always keep up with the latest trends, thus having to be more self-aware and grow up faster. Even with kids that aren't exposed to the internet that much, they are still influenced by their peers that do. I know it sounds kind of corny, but I wish kids had more time to just be kids and not feel the social pressure of knowing the latest trends and keeping appearances on social media.
