The New Turing Test

AI-generated images have become a big issue in the present day, becoming harder and harder to distinguish, with lots of people falling for them often. As Ullendorf states, AI images are generated by taking averages of the pictures a model has been trained on. The picture of the bread Labrador is probably based on pictures of dogs with that common pose, color, etc.

Cyborgs vs. Rooms

Reading this article made me think of early computers. They were huge, taking up entire rooms and were operated by a seemingly endless numbr of switches and buttons. It inspires me to make some sort of artwork that combines that old timey computing feel with modern technology, an exhibition that you can walk through and interact with.

My website is a shifting house next to a river of knowledge. What could yours be?

I had never really thought of websites as fluid forms of art. I am used to adhering industry standards, working towards creating a great user experience for others, not necessarily myself. I've always seen websites as a solution to a problem, whether be it a company trying to sell something to a display of information.

Eno Media Response

Before the Eno documentary, I hadn't really thought of music as more than just creative - I thought music must originate from just talented people, and I felt that I could never do so. It was encouraging to hear that one of Eno's most commercially successful songs was made almost entirely by an electronic instrument that didn't require a specialization in an instrument that often takes a lot of time and money to master. I also liked how he made a song for a guy (or it was a band?) by playing a game in the studio. It has made me rethink the process of making music and the classical training needed to make music.

The Coming Age of Calm Technology Response

When reading this article, I was surprised by just how much the article predicted would happen. I was also amused by the names it used for tech at the time and for the future, such as "thin servers," "thin clients," and "handheld computers." I wonder if that's where bougie bruger joints got their alternative names for burgers. It was interesting how it predicted that tech would impact writing and electricity: we see it today with the spreading of all kinds of writing online, from the month's latest meme to company announcements that can cause their stock to rise or fall. We are also feeling the effect of the increased need for electricity with the rise of electricity costs in the US and the worstening climate caused by humans using non-renewable resources to meet that demand, increased drastically since the 90s. I see the idea of the multicast in many things today, such as video and music streaming and social media platforms like Discord.
